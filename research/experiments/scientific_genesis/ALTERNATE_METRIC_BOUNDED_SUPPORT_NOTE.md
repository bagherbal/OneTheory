## The demonstrated deficiency and retained engines

The original sparse cochains store exact Eisenstein coefficients. Their
normalizer removes a coefficient when it is exactly zero. A root-error
enclosure cannot be placed in that scalar container: a zero center with a
positive radius is not zero. The exact support evaluator also constructs
exact-field matrices rather than error enclosures.

`BoundedCoefficients` therefore attaches existing circular enclosures to
the **original** `OuterCechBasis` labels. It sums identical labels, retains
zero-center uncertain coefficients, and removes only center-zero,
radius-zero entries. It is immutable, precision-explicit, and is not a
physical cochain, new basis, or alternate bundle.

The original raw homotopy and deck operators are applied to exact unit
cochains. Their exact columns are extended linearly to bounded
coefficients. Perturbation columns are likewise obtained from the original
mixed operator. The original finite lifting series is reused; its optional
homotopy callback selects the same unit-column homotopy in bounded mode.
Default exact-cochain behavior is unchanged. No new homotopy, contracting
series, or geometric equation is supplied.

## Why the bounds enclose the actual section

For an exact linear operator `T` with original unit columns `T(e_b)`, a
cochain with coefficients `c_b` satisfies

```text
T(sum_b c_b e_b) = sum_b c_b T(e_b).
```

Replacing each `c_b` by a containing circular enclosure and performing
outward arithmetic contains every coefficient of that exact expression.
Collection by original labels is addition, so possible cancellations can
widen a bound but cannot exclude the true value. Independent correlations
are not assumed. No center-zero or small-radius pruning is allowed.

The existing finite-pole theorem encodes `x^m` as its retained negative
support times the evaluated positive coefficient. Raw incidence and
homotopy depend on that retained support, not positive magnitudes.
Polynomial arrows cannot create a pole at a previously regular coordinate.
Subtracting artificial source powers before encoding the target gives
the original arrow functional. The regular-u theorem applies separately.
These identities hold for every coordinate functional on the declared
invertible-pivot domain, not just the root-disk center. Their compositions
therefore remain enclosure-safe with uncertain coordinates.

The actual object/Koszul filtration ranges from zero through four; every
perturbation/homotopy iteration strictly raises it. It gives the existing
five-term bound without relying on numerical cancellations. Positive-radius
zero-center coefficients cannot invalidate structural termination.
An unexpected sixth term is an error, not a reason to truncate the result.

Each Reynolds channel uses the original deck coordinate substitution.
