## Exact discrete choices

choose A, value 6 chooses Bx, and value 7 chooses Bu. Their probabilities are
therefore 3/4, 1/8 and 1/8, exactly the intersection-derived probabilities.
Each ternary selector reads disjoint pairs from its own independent stream.
Pairs 00, 01 and 10 select 0, 1 and 2; pair 11 consumes two bits and continues
on that SAME stream. For each output, the probability of stopping at pair k

`4^(-k)`; finite exhaustion is not an output or a new draw. This arithmetic
rejection is independent of geometry. Rejection of numerical/geometric
failures is forbidden. A uses two independent ternary selectors to choose
one of nine complete roots; Bx/Bu use the appropriate one for three roots.

## Refinement and branch identity

Numerical failure returns an immutable pending request containing its entire
original address, policy and reason. A later request must extend every stream
prefix. It may increase declared work/precision, not replace a draw. After
admission, refinement must preserve the SAME selected ideal root, not the
position of a newly sorted center. Every new complete root disk is matched
bijectively into an old disk by strict projective containment. Same-chart
containment follows from `|c_new-c_old|+r_new < r_old`. A reciprocal-chart
disk is first inverted with the existing certified disk arithmetic; failure
to exclude zero leaves continuation unresolved. The old uniform certificate
then identifies the refined actual root uniquely on the nested input cell.
This is stronger than comparing small equation residuals.

If a continuation cannot be certified, the previous admitted draw remains
attached to the pending request. Any later refinement is matched against it,
not relabelled from scratch. The workflow never silently replaces a failed
request, drops it from a batch, or counts all correlated roots as IID draws.
It does not certify eventual termination for all inputs or global numerical
coverage. Selecting only admitted draws would bias a numerical integral.
